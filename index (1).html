Log 02 · Materials engineeringMay – Aug 2026 · Glendale, CA

Glenair

Materials Engineering Intern, Materials & Process Group

What it is

Glenair makes connectors and interconnect hardware for aerospace and defense. The Materials & Process group owns the plating and finishing on those parts, the thin metal coatings that protect them from corrosion and wear.

What I did

01Root-causing a plating defect

Electroless-nickel-plated brass connector shells were coming out with a surface pattern defect. I scribed traceability coupons and pulled them at each step of the plating line, then imaged every stage. That narrowed nine possible failure points down to one, and the cause turned out to be human error, not the process. I presented the findings to 100+ senior leaders and interns.

02Identifying surface contaminants

Used SEM and EDS to find out what was actually sitting on the surface of plated shells, down to its elemental composition.

03Designing experiments

Set up a design of experiments for tin-zinc barrel plating, and built a 128-run test matrix covering connector parts across eight different finishes.

04Galvanic compatibility test plan

For my final project, I wrote a test plan for checking which finishes corrode each other when they touch, written to the MIL-DTL-38999 connector standard.

Log 03 · Research hardwareJun 2026 – Present · Santa Clarita

VORTEX

Mechanical Team · Visualization of Relativistic Tidal EXtremes

What it is

A tabletop analog black hole, built by a College of the Canyons physics club. Water drains through a vortex. Where the inward flow gets faster than surface waves can travel, waves can't escape, which behaves like an event horizon.

What I'm doing

Choosing the drain mechanism

The size of the drain controls the horizon, so we need to change it on the fly. I compared iris apertures, butterfly valves, and other designs, and chose a hollow-stem plunger.

Three design iterations

Modeled three versions of the plunger in SolidWorks, varying the cone angle and radius, and coordinated with the electrical team so it fits their controls.

Tank and actuator

Picked acrylic for the tank (clear for imaging, and it can be solvent-welded), and I'm evaluating stepper motors to drive the plunger.

Tools

SolidWorksPlunger and drain modeling
NEMA 17 stepper + TMC2209 driverActuator under evaluation
Log 04 · Space policyJan – May 2026 · Berkeley

Astroscale U.S.

Policy Strategy Consultant (Contract), through NewSpace at Berkeley

What it is

Astroscale builds spacecraft that capture and remove dead satellites and debris from orbit, which is called Active Debris Removal (ADR). The problem: in the U.S., no single agency clearly licenses these missions.

What I did

Mapped who's in charge

Oversight is split across NASA, the FCC, the FAA, and NOAA. I showed how that creates a principal-agent problem and a free-rider problem that discourage companies from investing in ADR.

Proposed a fix

Designed a three-phase strategy (consolidated authorization, legislative reform, and standardized regulatory language) projected to cut licensing timelines by 33%.

Designed enforcement

Built a tiered fine structure from $150K to $10M by violation severity, benchmarked against FCC enforcement precedent.

Sources & deliverables

Space Policy Directive-3U.S. space traffic management policy
National Orbital Debris Implementation PlanFederal debris strategy
DeliverablesWritten policy report and slide deck
